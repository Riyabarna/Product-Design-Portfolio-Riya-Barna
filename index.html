Process

How I work, and where AI helps.

The same five steps run through every case study above. AI is a tool inside them, and each use below ties to something I actually made.

Step 1

Frame

Name the problem, the user and the metric before opening Figma.

Step 2

Audit

Tear down 3 to 4 competing apps: steps, taps and drop-off points.

Step 3

Prototype

Figma for flows and visuals; AI coding tools for interactive states frames can't show.

Step 4

Test

Timed walkthroughs with peers, watching where they hesitate.

Step 5

Revise

Log what changed and why, then update the design.

Real data and microcopy instead of lorem ipsum

Layouts break when real Indian names, long college titles or changing surge prices replace placeholder text. I use LLMs while wireframing to generate realistic data variations and to stress-test tight labels.

In practiceIn OppFlow, I tightened the apply drawer and empty-filter copy into scannable 5 to 7 word prompts, and checked how cards held up with long company names and "Closes in 6 hrs" tags. In VibeAgent, I tested several phrasings for the "Why this was picked" tag so the AI felt transparent without crowding a mobile player.

Vibe coding state changes before locking the UI

Instead of linking dozens of duplicate Figma screens to test conditional logic, I use AI prototyping and coding tools to spin up interactive flows and see how layouts behave across viewports.

In practiceFor Blinkit's ingredient search, static mockups couldn't show how it feels to uncheck salt and oil and watch the bundle total update. Testing interactive states helped me check tap targets, list height and the single add-to-cart action before fixing the visual hierarchy.

Synthesis for audits and funnel hypotheses

Before Figma, I use AI to organise raw teardown notes into comparison matrices, spot drop-off triggers and define measurable north-star KPIs.

In practiceFor FareCompare, I mapped the full route-to-learning-loop architecture and logged pricing spreads, peak surge windows and deep-link paths across Uber, Ola, Rapido and Namma Yatri / ONDC, driving the decision to deep-link into native apps and compare fare + ETA side by side.

Before I designed screens, I designed carbon-fibre chassis parts and a VTOL payload carrier, where every gram and every load path had to be justified.

That habit carries over. I start with the constraint: a deadline, a drop-off point, a tap budget. Then I check the design against it with the people who will use it.

Constraints are inputs

I validated a payload carrier to a minimum safety factor of 15 in ANSYS while holding net weight to 0.20 kg. I treat front-end and data limits the same way, as part of the brief.
